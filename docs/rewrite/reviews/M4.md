1. **High — INTENDED dispatch policy: define when nonzero dispatch is required and which rule wins.** The document describes CURRENT follow behavior but provides no intended contract for reserve hold, peak discharge, self-consumption, maintenance, or their precedence against safety and import limits. Thus, the observed all-day `0 W / verified` outcome remains contract-compliant even with usable energy during expensive periods. **Required change:** specify eligibility, targets, limits, and precedence for each mode; add acceptance scenarios that distinguish justified holds from failures to dispatch. Do not equate successful execution of a zero command with satisfaction of the energy objective.

2. **High — INTENDED receipt contract: separate write confirmation, measured flow, and verified outcome.** “Two fresh reports of real power” does not define successful holds, freshness, distinct samples, minimum power, direction tolerance, or whether samples must follow the relevant command. `partial_flow` is also listed among “other” outcomes without stating whether it can be verified. This permits inconsistent success reporting or rejection of legitimate holds. **Required change:** define an outcome table covering hold, full flow, partial flow, echo-only, read failure, and no-flow. Require per-pack evidence associated with the relevant command; define how holds are confirmed without claiming nonzero flow.

3. **High — Write failures and backoff: specify the standing-command lifecycle and fail-safe acknowledgement.** CURRENT promises one fail-safe hold and safety bypass, but INTENDED does not say what happens if that hold fails or remains unconfirmed, nor how the original command, fallback write, and standing command are reported. A pack could continue charging or discharging while the row suggests a successful stop. **Required change:** report each pack’s requested command, write acknowledgement, confirmed standing command, and fallback outcome separately; define bounded retries/escalation for unconfirmed safety holds and acceptance tests for mixed fleet acknowledgements.

4. **Medium — Ramp and partial-flow pause: resolve timing and interruption rules.** The only explicit transition duration is CURRENT’s 120 seconds; the requested 45-second ramp is unspecified. The 120-second partial-flow pause also lacks release conditions and explicit interruption rules for changed targets, reserve protection, or safety stops. This can delay needed action or preserve an obsolete command. **Required change:** define the 45-second ramp, its interaction with the 30 W deadband and verification timeout, and which events interrupt or restart the partial-flow pause.

5. **Medium — Reporting schema: make unavailable flow distinguishable from measured zero.** The document identifies `written = true` with missing `applied_w` as a defect but does not establish a complete replacement invariant. Advisory mode sets `applied_w = 0`, which may not represent actual flow from an already-standing command. Backoff measurements likewise need freshness and command attribution. **Required change:** make `applied_w` exclusively a fresh, signed per-pack measurement; represent unavailable measurements explicitly rather than as zero. Define independent meanings for `written`, `verified`, and `flow_measured` across live, advisory, failed-read, pause, and backoff rows.

**Verdict: approve with required changes.** The supplied contract does not yet rule out doing nothing while reporting execution success, or claiming success from evidence unrelated to the current command.
