1. **High — `validation.rs::validate_timeline/check_slot`: Discontinuous or incomplete timelines can pass as healthy.** Each slot is checked independently; adjacent energies, timestamps, horizon coverage, and slot count are never reconciled. S19 even resets energy from 3.0675 to 3.0 without a discharge. Validate continuity, ordering, and complete horizon coverage before accepting the final timeline.

2. **High — `validation.rs::check_powers/check_slot`: Aggregate commands are not reconciled with pack commands or site power balance.** Energy conservation uses `battery_w`, while power limits use only `pack_powers`; neither their equality nor the grid/load/PV balance is checked. An excessive aggregate command with empty pack powers can evade the clamp checks. Validate both balances and enforce aggregate limits directly on the final battery command.

3. **High — `validation.rs::check_obligation`: Missing required assignments are downgraded to optional-style degradation.** The missing-slot branch always sets `degraded` and returns, even when `class == Required`. A required outcome can therefore become eligible for degraded dispatch instead of being infeasible. Mark missing required assignments as hard violations; retain degradation only for optional obligations.

4. **High — `bounds.rs::validate` and `validation.rs`: Non-finite numerical inputs can silently pass validation.** Power bounds accept NaN/infinity, efficiencies accept zero, and timeline values are not checked for finiteness. NaN energy makes floor, ceiling, conservation, and terminal-shortfall comparisons fail open. Reject non-finite physical inputs and require strictly positive efficiencies before evaluating feasibility.

5. **High — `validation.rs::check_obligation`: Obligation completion is trusted rather than validated against the final maintained timeline.** Except for the `grid_charge` headroom cap, feasibility uses the supplied `available_kwh` without establishing delivery to the named pack by its deadline. Maintenance or clamping can remove an obligation’s delivery while its stale availability still produces a healthy plan. Derive or reconcile availability against final per-pack delivery through the deadline.

6. **Medium — `validation.rs::check_terminal`: The 0.05 kWh deadband is not deducted from reported shortfall.** The implementation reports the full deficit and only uses 0.05 as a pass/fail threshold. For target 1.178625 and final energy 1.100000, it reports 0.078625 rather than the deductible shortfall of 0.028625 kWh. Compute reportable shortfall as `max(target - final_energy - 0.05, 0)` and test exact boundary values.

7. **Medium — `validation.rs::check_obligation` and `m3_maintenance.rs::s22`: Unassigned-pack reporting replaces the required named reason.** S22 supplies `horizon_too_short`, but validation replaces it with `assignment_slot_missing`, and the test explicitly requires that replacement. Preserve the specified unassigned reason (`no_headroom`, `horizon_too_short`, `soc_ceiling_below_target`, or `shortfall`); record a missing assignment slot separately.

8. **Medium — `m3_states.rs` / `m3_maintenance.rs`: The supplied scenario tests do not establish several frozen requirements.** S75 invokes assembly on an already-built timeline, so it cannot prove refusal before solving; S78 uses an in-horizon deadline and forces optionality through `eligible: false`, so it does not test its claimed outside-horizon case; S23 is absent. Add a pre-solve refusal test, a genuinely outside-horizon deadline case, and the missing S23 coverage.
